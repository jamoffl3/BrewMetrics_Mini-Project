The BrewMetrics dataset contains 15,482 sales transactions across four cities: Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, and Coimbatore, covering April to July 2026. The total sales were approximately ₹40.66 lakh, with Bengaluru recording the highest sales at ₹11.20 lakh, followed by Chennai at ₹10.55 lakh, Hyderabad at ₹9.69 lakh, and Coimbatore at ₹8.23 lakh. Cold Brew showed a clear seasonal pattern, with sales increasing from ₹2.77 lakh in April to ₹3.01 lakh in May, followed by a decline to ₹1.85 lakh in June. July contains only one day of data, so it should not be directly compared with the complete months. These statistics helped identify city-level performance differences and the seasonal behaviour of Cold Brew. Working on the project with GitHub Copilot also helped me understand how AI assistance can support Power BI development, particularly by providing initial suggestions for DAX measures such as month-over-month growth, running totals, and product rankings. However, the generated formulas had to be checked against the actual data model, table names, relationships, and business requirements. Using Git also made the development process more structured by allowing the schema, measures, dashboard, and documentation to be tracked through separate commits. Overall, the project demonstrated how data analysis, Power BI visualization, AI assistance, and version control can be combined to create a more organized and traceable Business Intelligence solution.
